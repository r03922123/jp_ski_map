**ODbL note.** The OpenStreetMap-derived files stay under ODbL, and any database built from them falls under its share-alike terms ([ODbL](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/) §4.4); maps and other Produced Works made from them don't (§4.5.b). That doesn't change the licence of the code that reads them. `stop_evidence.json` states the notice in its `_doc` field; the other JSON files carry none of their own, so this file is the notice.

**Resorts, bus stops and stop evidence.** ODbL covers each of these three files as a whole, not only its OpenStreetMap rows ([ADR-0002](docs/adr/0002-data-files-odbl.md)). In `bus_stops.json` and `stop_evidence.json` one coordinate field mixes OpenStreetMap values with values from elsewhere. That falls outside the safe harbour of the OSMF's [Collective Database Guideline](https://osmfoundation.org/wiki/Licence/Community_Guidelines/Collective_Database_Guideline_Guideline), so share-alike can reach the whole file ([ODbL](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/) §4.4.a, §4.4.b). The coordinates in `resorts.json` are all from OpenStreetMap; it gets the same label by choice, so one rule covers all three files. From OpenStreetMap: 50 of the 100 stops (22 OSM points, 8 from the lodging snapshot, 7 via Photon, 13 resort access points), all 12 resort access points, and 18 of the 66 evidence entries. Two groups are named but not covered. They had no clearly matching OpenStreetMap feature when checked on 2026-11-05, and follow-up 1 stays open until another source is chosen for them:

- **47 Google My Maps pins:** the stops whose `coordSource` starts with `operator My Maps pin`, and the same 47 entries in `stop_evidence.json`. Copied from the bus operator's Google My Maps; their licence is unresolved. In `stop_evidence.json` these pins sit under a key named `osm`, but they are not OpenStreetMap data: their `ref` starts with `operator My Maps pin`.
- **1 GSI-geocoded stop:** the stop whose `coordSource` starts with `gsi:`. Geocoded through the address search of Japan's Geospatial Information Authority (GSI). GSI's answer on [gsi-cyberjapan/gsimaps#29](https://github.com/gsi-cyberjapan/gsimaps/issues/29) (2015) says that search answers address queries with CSIS Simple Geocoding (University of Tokyo), whose [terms](https://geocode.csis.u-tokyo.ac.jp/home/simple-geocoding/simple-geocoding-tems-of-use/) ask for a credit on maps and in apps (第4条(1), (4)). Its entry in `stop_evidence.json` holds only the street address, taken from a hotel booking page.

The other 2 stops have no coordinates yet (`coord` is `null`).

**Test fixture.** `apps/web/e2e/fixtures/bus-options-2026-03-20.json` is a captured bus-options API response that the end-to-end tests serve in place of the live API, so it holds copies of stop coordinates from `bus_stops.json`. For 4 stops those copies are Google My Maps pins, and they fall under the My Maps exception above: `stop-3-parking`, `stop-cook` and `stop-echoland-spicy`, and the old pin of `stop-snowlines-lodge`, which [PR #17](https://github.com/r03922123/jp_ski_map/pull/17) replaced in `bus_stops.json`. The fixture is left as captured, because regenerating it needs the running API.

**Timetables.** The operators' original PDFs and photos are kept out of the repository. Only the transcribed departure times are committed. They may be out of date or contain mistakes, so always check the official timetable before you travel.
